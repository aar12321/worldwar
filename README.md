# Worlds of Others

A browser-based, turn-based grand strategy game of total global hegemony. You command one of 175 nations; every other nation is run by an AI rival, or by a friend: one to four people can play the same world online. One turn is one month, starting January 2026.

## Quick start

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test           # engine unit tests (Vitest)
npm run build      # type-check + production bundle
```

## How to play

The game has two modes, chosen when you start. In **Turns** (the default), one turn is one month, starting January 2026, and nothing moves until you press **Next turn** (or Enter). In **Live** (for one player), time flows by itself and you watch armies march: see [Live mode](#live-mode).

1. Pick a nation on the main menu (search the list or click the globe), and choose how skilled your rivals are. **You win by holding the whole world**: every country yours or a puppet of yours. It is a long game, built for about 12 to 15 hours of play. Taxes start at the neutral 20% (and business tax at 15%), so every change you make has an effect. **Easy** rivals play the basics, wait a long time before attacking you, and never band together against you. **Medium** rivals also grow cities, set budgets and laws, fund business, and form alliances and blocs. **Hard** rivals also borrow, invest abroad, sanction, counter the units you field, press an ally's enemy together, and turn a crushing win into a puppet state.
   **Scenarios.** Under *Scenario* on the main menu you can start somewhere other than the Standard world: a cold war between two blocs, a tiny country among giants, a peaceful builder's start, an arms race, a neighbor that collapses, a resource crunch, a plague year, a strait under fire, a rising superpower, a war on two fronts, a powder keg. Each has a plain-words briefing, its own goal (some win by surviving, growing, or out-earning; some use the usual goals) and a suggested nation and rival skill, which you can ignore. The Briefing desk shows the scenario's goal and how far along you are.
2. The game is run from **six desks** on the left (Briefing, Economy, Military, Diplomacy, Empire, Research):
   - **Briefing** (`B`): your goal, a first-steps checklist for new players, and your advisor's list of what needs you this month. Every item has a button that takes you to the fix.
   - **Economy** (`E`): nine tabs (see [Statecraft](#statecraft) and [Caring for the people](#caring-for-the-people-and-building-for-the-future)): *People* is where you look after your country and *Projects* is where you build for its future. *Bank* is the central bank, *Budget* lists every money line, and *Government* has law slots. *Country* shows **your whole empire in one list** (every country you hold, its trait, how settled it is, and a **governor** who upgrades it each month on a plan you choose), then grows your cities (each takes a focus that boosts the whole country: industry, farming, harbor, research, fortress, finance) and buys Factory, Farm, University, Hospital, Barracks, Port and Civic Hall upgrades. *Budget* shows where money comes from and goes (every line, adding up to what is left over) and sets income tax and the draft; bonds, printing and interest rates are in *Bank*. *Market* buys and sells food, oil, steel and goods at world prices, within a monthly trade limit that ports raise, with auto-buy for shortages. *Stocks* is the exchange: made-up companies whose prices follow the economy, stability, taxes and troubles of their home countries, with dividends, crashes and rallies. You can only invest in companies in countries you hold, and a very developed country has no more than two. Buy shares to take a stake (10% investor, 25% board seat, 51% control, 75% dominant; big trades move the price against you). With a board seat you instruct the company: **pay dividends**, **expand the business** (its sector grows faster) or **specialize** (technology: research and cheaper Frontier systems; arms: cheaper units and upkeep; finance, energy, manufacturing and farming each lift the nation), at half strength with a board seat and full strength with control. Take a country in war and its companies, with your old owner's shares in them, are yours. *Business* has six private sectors that grow by themselves (set the business tax, give grants, subsidize, invest abroad). *Government* picks democracy, monarchy, junta or one-party rule and passes laws, within the room your empire has for them.
   - **Military** (`M`): four tabs. *Systems* runs the Frontier programs (below). *Agencies* runs your intelligence service (the CIA in the USA) abroad and your security service (the FBI) at home: gather intelligence, build a case for war, sabotage, arm rebels, help a friend keep order, steal technology, back a coup on a shaky nation, and (with Cyber Warfare) cyber attack. Odds come from agency level, experience, technology, partners and the target's own security, and are shown before you commit. A noticed failure costs reputation and hands the target a reason to fight you. *Forces* trains infantry, tanks, artillery, fighters, bombers and warships (they arrive after 1 to 3 turns; vehicles cost steel and burn oil, and fighters decide how much bombers can do), buys permanent weapon levels and drill, and appoints a commander. Every unit shows its attack and defense and where each bonus comes from; battles use exactly those numbers. *Wars* (`W`) lists your wars (three at most), their monthly bills and each front's Defense Line.
   - **Diplomacy** (`D`): offers waiting on you, deals, and every neighbor's opinion of you with pacts, alliances, guarantees, trade, loans, investment, sanctions and peace terms, including making a defeated nation a puppet that pays tribute. A peace offer can **include the allies and puppets on both sides**, so one signing ends every war between them. The **Wars** tab lists each front weakest first with your odds, and *Make peace* opens the peace terms directly. Another nation's strength is a rounded **guess** until you are allied, at war, hold it as a puppet or have gathered intelligence on it.
   - **Empire** (`I`): what your size opens up, how stretched your officials are, how you hold conquered land, your capitals and the World Council.
   - **Research** (`R`): spend research on technologies, in seven branches (the seventh is Medicine), and spare research on lasting programs. The **Frontier** branch holds the milestones: combat drones, cyber warfare, missile defense, artificial intelligence, hypersonic missiles, robotics, quantum computing, orbital systems and nanotechnology. Each unlocks something real, with a counter: **Combat Drones** (a unit that takes the first losses and shreds tanks and guns; shot down by fighters and shields), **Robot Infantry** (no soldiers needed, their losses never tire your people; hacked by a higher Cyber Command), **Hypersonic Missiles** (strike first in their own opening round and crack the Defense Line directly; you choose how many to fire in a battle, or fire any number in strikes at a country within 6000 km, as often as you have missiles; each one fired is used up; an Interceptor Shield stops a share), and six systems bought in three levels with upkeep: **Cyber Command**, **Interceptor Shield**, **AI Battle Command** (more attacks a month, exact odds, a stance recommendation), **Quantum Codebreaking** (warnings of where an enemy could strike), **Orbital Network** (intelligence on enemies, easier landings, one orbital strike a year) and **Nano-armor** (less damage, part of every loss comes back). The strikes are in the War Room.
   - News (`N`), help (`?`) and settings sit in the top-left bar.
3. **War needs a reason.** A great power cannot just attack a neighbor. A reason comes from a border incident, an attack on your ally or a country you guarantee, a rival caught spying on you, sanctions against you, standing with a victim in a crisis, an operation by your intelligence service, or a **case** you build yourself (20 influence, ready in 3 months). With a reason, war costs 10 influence. Without one it costs 60, drops your stability, and the world distrusts you: every attack and captured country adds to your **reputation for aggression**, which makes other nations uneasy until it fades over a couple of years. Rivals follow the same rules.
   **The news** arrives in stages, never early: a rumor, a disturbance, then a crisis (unrest in a country, or one nation threatening another). You can send aid, mediate, or back the victim, or ignore it. Left alone it may boil over into uprisings or war, and allies may call you to join.
   **War is a campaign.** Click a neighboring country and press **Declare war**. You can attack across a border or a short sea lane; a port plus warships reaches any coast in the world, and fighters or strike aircraft with a factory as an airbase reach anything within 7000 km (these fight with a landing penalty). Then press **Plan an attack** (`A`) to open the War Room: choose how much of your military to send (Probe, Main force, All in, or any share from 5% to 100% with *Choose*) and how to fight (Assault, Blitz or Siege). The room shows honest odds, expected losses and what the war costs. Each win pushes back the country's **Defense Line**; when it reaches zero the country falls. Big countries and capitals have long lines, small ones short. Lines heal a little in months nobody attacks them.
   - **Units you send are away until the turn ends**, so your home army fights at reduced strength and rivals can punish an all-in attack.
   - **Every war costs a monthly bill** that grows with your military and the enemy's size, and tires your people. A small country can be in three wars at once, and one more for every 12 countries it holds (up to eight). Your military can also launch more attacks a month as the empire grows: three, plus one for every six countries (up to nine). Training slots, influence and the number of unsettled countries you can hold also grow with the empire. A nation whose capital has fallen and whose army is gone, or under a fifth of yours, surrenders whole. A puppet fights beside you in every war, and after a year it can be **annexed** (15 influence for each of its countries): its land, army and treasury join yours. No nation costs more than 80 war score to make a puppet, however big.
   - **Every country has a trait** (chip foundries, financial hub, energy giant, rare minerals, breadbasket, mountain stronghold...) that helps your whole nation once it settles. When a nation falls its conqueror also takes 40% of its treasury and learns a technology it knew.
   - **Conquered land starts restless** and produces about 40% at first, settling over six months. Holding many unsettled countries strains stability.
4. **Next turn** runs the rivals, training, research, income, diplomacy, rebels and any attacks on you, replays the battles you were in, then shows a **monthly report**: what changed, the news, and what the advisor would do next.
5. The **map control** (bottom left) switches the globe between Nations, Relations (allies, pacts, enemies) and Fronts (how much Defense Line is left).

## Statecraft

Beyond war, you now rule a country's money, people and industry, and what you can do grows with your empire.

- **Empire tiers.** Holding 4, 10, 25 and 60 countries (a puppet counts half) makes you a **Regional power**, **Great power**, **Superpower** and **Hegemon**. Each tier opens or widens: your own interest rate, how much you can print, bond terms, war bonds, directed credit, a wealth fund, national projects, state enterprises, law slots (3 to 7), how far schools can specialize, the military academy, arms exports, occupation policies, capitals, bigger World Bank loans and the World Council. You keep a tier until you fall a tenth below its line. The top bar names your tier; the Empire desk shows what the next one adds.
- **People.** Your workers are basic workers, skilled workers, engineers, doctors and scientists. **Schools** (spending 0 to 4, a stage and a focus) decide the mix and how educated people are; a focus **specializes** the people over two years. Factories run on engineers, universities on scientists, hospitals on doctors: the People tab shows how well each is staffed. Only people with **jobs** pay tax. **Health** (spending, hospitals, homes, medicine) decides life expectancy, growth and calm. **Welfare** (pensions, jobless support, housing) buys stability and an easier election. People **migrate** toward a better life, as your immigration policy allows. **Epidemics** start where health is poor, cross borders and ease with doctors, vaccines and a quarantine. The People tab is described in full [below](#caring-for-the-people-and-building-for-the-future).
- **Money.** From a Regional power you run a **central bank**: a higher rate cools prices, a lower one warms them. **Printing** money gives cash now and inflation later; it never pays for itself. A **currency** moves with rates, inflation, credit and war. **Bonds** run 1, 3 or 5 years at the rate fixed when sold, and are rolled over or repaid when due. **War bonds**, **directed credit** and a **wealth fund** open as you grow; the largest economy of a Superpower or Hegemon holds the **reserve currency**. Dividends follow the world interest rate, investments run down as they pay, and a corruption sweep finds less the sooner it follows the last, so none of it is free money.
- **Industry.** **Public works** spending sets trade, factories, wall repairs and building prices. Seventeen **national projects** take months and engineers, and each can be built to three levels. The state can buy or **seize** a business sector. How big a sector can grow depends on the skills of your people. Projects are described in full [below](#caring-for-the-people-and-building-for-the-future).
- **Empire.** Bonuses from many countries add up with diminishing returns, so a vast empire no longer grows with the square of its size. Your officials can run only so many countries (**administration**); beyond it, stability and output fall. Civic halls, governors, **capitals and regional capitals** help. **Occupation policy** sets how conquered land settles. A Superpower can put **World Council** resolutions to a weighted vote.
- **Military and research.** An **academy** (levels 1 to 3) sends out drilled recruits and sharpens combined arms. Doctors return part of the wounded infantry to the ranks. Commanders have traits (mountains, logistics, blitz, air, defense). **Arms exports** earn money at a cost in goodwill. Spare research buys **future programs** that raise output, health or weapons.
- **Twelve new events** (bank run, inflation riot, brain drain, doctors' strike, medical breakthrough, collapsed bridge, housing boom, pension crisis, refugees, currency attack, council deadlock, project overruns) bring these systems into your decisions.
- Older saves load: the old budget becomes spending levels, the healthcare law becomes health spending, and debt becomes a bond.

## Caring for the people, and building for the future

Money is only worth what you do with it. Two Economy tabs are where it goes.

### People

The **People** tab has seven sections: *Overview*, *Health*, *Learning*, *Work*, *Family*, *Safety* and *Campaigns*.

- **Why stability is heading where it is.** The Overview lists every cause of stability with its points: taxes, hunger, empty shops, debt, war weariness, government and laws, technology, rebels, stretched officials, health and welfare, prices, epidemics, conquered land, immigration and your programs. Add them to the starting calm and you get where stability is heading, so you know which lever to pull.
- **22 programs in five groups.** Health and care (community clinics, clean water, mental health, elder care), Learning (school meals, scholarships, apprenticeships, libraries), Work (minimum wage, a public jobs guarantee, small-business loans, workers' rights), Family and homes (child benefit, parental leave, food assistance, public transport) and Safety and unity (community policing, courts and anti-corruption, integration, festivals, public broadcasting, state surveillance). Each is **Off, Pilot, National or Universal** (Universal needs a Regional power).
  - Raising a program costs **influence** for each step, then **money every month** by its level and the size of your workforce. Its effect **phases in over a few months** and fades out the same way. **Cutting** one costs stability at once, because people do not forgive losing what they were given. A program can change once a month.
  - Programs feed the real numbers: health, schooling, jobs, births, the stability needed to win an election, how likely rebels are to rise, how fast conquered land settles, epidemics, how hard shortages bite, and how many countries your officials can run. Several carry a real trade-off: minimum wage slows business, state surveillance drives scientists away and democracies punish it, workers' rights slow factories, parental leave costs output.
  - Stability from care has a **ceiling** (18 points at the very most), so the first programs help most and nobody buys their way to perfect calm.
  - **Calm costs; growth pays.** Measured by simulating five years, the programs for growth (courts, public transport, small-business loans, apprenticeships) earn back roughly two thirds to nine tenths of their cost in income, plus research and jobs. Programs for calm are a pure cost.
- **7 campaigns.** A national celebration, a vaccination drive, a literacy campaign, a job fair, a unity tour of the provinces, a food bank drive and a baby bonus. Each is a one-off bill (by the size of your country) for a burst of effect, with a long wait before the next.

### Projects

The **Projects** tab has two views.

- **National.** Seventeen projects, grouped by the size of empire that opens them. The first seven are rail, power grid, deep-water ports, science park, medical research centre, digital government and a space launch complex. The new ten are a clean water network, a national stadium, national broadband, a strategic grain reserve, an open university, a World Expo (every nation thinks better of its host), a national AI lab, a fusion power plant, a planned capital city and a Mars mission.
  - Every project can be built to **three levels**. Each level costs and takes more, and is worth more but not in proportion, so the first level is the best deal. The levels you have keep working while the next is built. Level 3 needs at least a Regional power. Upkeep is a share of everything put in.
  - You can build 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 at once as a Nation, Regional power, Great power, Superpower or Hegemon. **Engineers set the pace**, so building many at once slows them all.
  - **Rush** a project: pay four months of its bill to bring it two months closer, once a month for each project.
- **In your countries.** Each country you hold can have its own projects, built up to three levels, and they **stay with the land** if it is conquered (a half-built one is dropped). There are nine: an industrial park, a tech hub (needs a university), irrigation, a hydroelectric dam (needs mountains or forest), a tourist resort (needs a coast), a cultural centre (conquered land settles faster, and rebels rarer), a border fortress, a special economic zone (needs a port) and a mine expansion (needs deposits). A bigger country costs more to build in and gives more back, so the return is much the same wherever you build. You can build 2, 3, 5, 7 or 10 at once by empire size.

## Live mode

Pick **Live** on the start screen and the same game runs on a clock: a month takes about a minute at normal speed (a day every two seconds), with pause, 2x and 4x. A new game starts paused; **Space** plays or pauses, **1**, **2** and **3** set the speed. Everything else works as in Turns: the economy, research, diplomacy, agencies, crises, stocks, Frontier systems, governors and bulk upgrades.

- **Orders.** Upgrades, training, trades, shares and war work at once. Research, taxes, the draft and offers to other nations wait for the 1st of the next month, and the Orders bar says when. Answering an offer, such as accepting peace, counts at once.
- **Armies.** An attack in the War Room sends an army out of your military. It leaves from a border country, a port or an airbase, marches for days (by land, across a sea lane, by fleet or by air: the War Room shows the days and the dates), fights with the usual combat rules when it arrives, stays a day, and marches home; the survivors rejoin your military. Units that are away do not defend your country, but still cost upkeep. Recall an army while it is on its way out, or turn it back by making peace.
- **On the globe.** Each army is a badge in its nation's color (a plane, ship, tank or infantry mark). Yours have a white outline and a dashed blue route; enemy armies marching on you are outlined in red with a red route, a pulsing ring over the country and an alert in the top bar with the days left. Battles show a pin with the Defense Line draining and the result; hypersonic missiles fly as arcs and land a day later; an orbital strike hits at once.
- **The AI** plans once a month as in Turns, and its attacks leave on days of the month, so you see them coming.
- **What stops the clock:** pausing, a decision (an event), a battle window (opt in with Settings > Pause on my battles), the monthly report if you chose that, the War Room, and the help window. The clock never runs in the background of a hidden tab. Live games are single player; playing with friends online uses Turns.

## Play with friends (1 to 4 players)

On the main menu press **Play with friends online**, type a name, and **Create a room**. You get a five-letter code. Friends press the same button, choose **Join a room** and type it. In the lobby everyone picks a nation, and the host sets the rules: rival skill (Easy, Medium, Hard), how you win (hold the whole world, or a short, standard or long game of countries taken, people held or share of the world's output), and how long each month lasts (no timer, 1, 1.5, 2 or 3 minutes). Nations nobody picks are run by the AI, and AI nations still ally, trade and fight each other.

- **Everyone plans the same month at once.** Press **Ready** when you are done. The month moves on when every player is ready, or when the timer ends. Press the button again to keep planning. Players who are away are skipped, and their nation is run by the AI until they return.
- **Attacks wait for the end of the month online.** In a one-player game an attack plays out the moment you order it. Online, it joins your plan and is resolved at month end in a seeded order, so nobody wins by clicking faster. Builds, trades and training still happen at once.
- **Surprise decisions come to each player separately** and never hold the month. Choose when you like; the answer travels with your plan. If you do not choose, your ministers take the first option when the month ends.
- The first player to reach the goal wins, and the end screen names the winner and shows every player's countries, people and strength. The host can offer a **Rematch**. A player whose nation falls keeps watching; the game ends when someone wins or every player has fallen.
- Open **Players and chat** under the Ready button to see who is ready (the Ready box says who the month is waiting on), to talk, and (as host) to remove a player. Each player has their own color. Offers between players wait in the other player's inbox until the next month begins, and you get a note when one arrives.
- **If the host presses Leave or reloads, the next player takes over at once**, in the lobby or in a game. If the host's tab simply closes or their connection dies, the others notice within about 12 seconds and the next player takes over. A screen whose own connection has dropped says **Reconnecting** and never crowns itself host; when the line returns it asks for the game and sends its plan again.
- **Saved games.** After every month each player's device keeps the room (squeezed to about 60 KB, the last three rooms). If everyone closes their tab, any player opens it again from **Saved games** under Play with friends, choosing which nation to take, and the others join with the same code and take their seats back. A player on a new device takes the seat of someone who is away the same way.
- There is no undo online, since your orders are already on their way to the host.

### Connecting players

The room code is shared through [Supabase Realtime](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime) broadcast. No app server, database tables or accounts are needed, only a project's URL and public publishable key, which the browser reads at build time:

1. Create a free project at supabase.com. Realtime broadcast is on by default.
2. Copy **Project URL** and the **publishable key** from *Project Settings, API*.
3. For local use put them in `.env.local` (see `.env.example`). For a deployed site set `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` where the site is built, then rebuild and republish. The app also accepts `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`. Vite bakes these public values into the browser bundle.

```bash
cp .env.example .env.local   # then edit the two values
npm run dev
```

Without them the game still runs, and **Play with friends** works in a practice mode between tabs of the same browser (open the page twice). It cannot connect different computers.

How it works: the host's browser runs the same pure engine as a one-player game and is the referee. Other players send their plan for the month to the host, who checks every order against that player's own nation, resolves the month, and sends the new game back (compressed, in pieces under 50 KB). Everyone's game is therefore identical. The room is a trust-your-friends design: the host's browser holds the truth, and there is no server-side anti-cheat. Messages are not encrypted beyond what Supabase provides, so do not share the room code with strangers.

Shortcuts: `Enter` next turn, `B` briefing, `E` economy, `M` military, `W` wars, `D` diplomacy, `R` research, `N` news, `A` War Room for the selected country, `?` help, `Esc` close.

## Architecture

```
src/
  engine/      Pure, deterministic simulation (no DOM). resolveTurn(state, map, orders) -> new state
    resolveTurn.ts  one month: month-end orders, training, attacks, rebels, economy, diplomacy, events (runMonth; resolveTurn wraps it)
    live.ts         the live clock: advanceDay(state, map, { monthOrders, planner }) moves a live game by one day, running the month on the last day
    armies.ts       live armies: launch point, march days, recall, the shortest-path position of an army at a given time
    calendar.ts     real month lengths and absolute days for the live clock
    orders.ts       validation and costs. Orders in MONTH_END_ORDERS wait for Next turn; the rest apply at once
    militaryStats.ts  the per-unit stat sheet and military power (battles and the AI read the same numbers)
    combat.ts / warfare.ts  3-round battles, whole-country capture, garrisons, rebels
    arms.ts / training.ts   weapon levels, training slots and queue
    economy.ts / diplomacy.ts / espionage.ts / tech.ts / victory.ts
    society.ts      social programs and campaigns: effects, the care bill, phasing in, orders
    works.ts        projects built in a country: levels, needs, size-scaled costs, rush
  data/        Tech tree, units, buildings, terrain, events, country stats, starting nations, scenarios (scenarios.ts, plain data applied by engine/scenarios.ts), social programs (society.ts), projects (projects.ts, localProjects.ts)
  ai/          Rule-based orders for every nation no human plays (same rules as the player): bot, diplomat, management, opinion, blocs, and strategy (each bot's monthly posture, target choice from real battle forecasts, force sizing, peace timing; see docs/STRATEGY.md)
  net/         Online play: protocol (messages, room codes, compressed snapshots), transport (Supabase Realtime with reconnect status, same-browser fallback), session (lobby, host terms and takeover, seats, ready, timer), saves (rooms kept on each device)
  map/world.ts Natural Earth 110m countries -> regions, adjacency, coastlines, sea lanes
  ui/          React: globe, HUD, drawers, Modal, battle window, diplomacy, tech tree. ui/globe has the army layer (badges, routes, missile arcs); ui/liveClock.ts is the one loop that runs a live game's clock
  store.ts     Zustand store: orders, turn flow, battle queue, autosave (localStorage)
```

The engine is seeded and side-effect free, so the same seed and orders always produce the same world, and a live game's days are deterministic too. Turn games carry none of the live fields, and a pinned test (`turnGolden.test.ts`) keeps a turn month exactly as it was. `GameState.humans` lists the nations people play (one in a one-player game), so a multiplayer game and a single-player game run the same code. Online, the host resolves each month and everyone else receives the result.

## Tech stack

Vite, React 19, TypeScript, Zustand, Framer Motion, Tailwind CSS v4, three.js via `react-globe.gl`, `world-atlas` + `topojson-client` + `d3-geo` for map data, Vitest, and `@supabase/supabase-js` for online rooms.
